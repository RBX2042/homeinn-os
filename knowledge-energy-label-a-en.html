Upgrading to energy label A: what it involves and what it delivers

HomeINN · Energy upgrades · 7 min read

Bringing a home up to energy label A sounds like a big job, and it is. It is rarely a matter of one measure, but of a coherent package: properly insulating the building envelope and then switching to a modern, efficient way of heating. For many Rotterdam property owners the question is not so much whether it can be done, but what it involves in practice, what it delivers and how you get it done without the budget running away in all directions.

In this article we calmly explain what a route to label A consists of, which benefits are realistic and how HomeINN tackles this together with a fixed construction partner: from shell to move-in ready, within a budget agreed in advance.

What does energy label A actually mean?

The energy label says something about how energy-efficient a home is, from the least efficient labels up to the efficient A segment and beyond. The better the label, the less energy a home needs to stay comfortable. In practice, label A is mainly the outcome of a well-insulated envelope and efficient installations that are matched to each other, rather than a goal in itself.

It is important to realise that the route to label A differs greatly from one property to another. A pre-war ground-floor flat in Oude Noorden calls for something quite different from a post-war family home in IJsselmonde. Year of construction, structure and current condition determine which measures make sense and in what order. An honest process therefore always starts with an inspection of the home, not with a standard package.

The envelope first: insulating the roof, walls, floor and glazing

The basis of every energy upgrade is insulation. As long as heat escapes unhindered, no installation really helps. That is why we focus first on the building envelope: the roof, the walls, the floor and the glazing. This is usually the most decisive step towards label A, and often also the step that most noticeably improves comfort.

Which elements take priority depends on the home. In many older Rotterdam properties, roof insulation and good glazing are a logical starting point, while wall and floor insulation depend on the structure and on the accessibility of, for example, a crawl space or cavity wall.

  • Roof: often a major source of heat loss and usually straightforward to tackle
  • Walls: cavity wall, internal or external wall insulation, depending on the wall type
  • Floor: ground or floor insulation, particularly worthwhile where there is a crawl space
  • Glazing: from single or outdated double glazing to modern insulating glass, sometimes with draught-proofing and ventilation to finish

Then the installations: heat pump and modern technology

Once the envelope is in order, the home is ready for an efficient way of heating. A well-insulated property needs less heat, and that is precisely when a heat pump comes into its own: it works best at a lower flow temperature, which goes well with an insulated home and, for example, larger radiators or underfloor heating.

Alongside the heat pump there are often other installations that make the whole work: a form of balanced or demand-controlled ventilation to keep the air healthy in a more airtight home, and sometimes solar panels to generate part of your own consumption. Whether a full switch is feasible and sensible, or a hybrid intermediate step is more logical, depends on the property and your plans for it. That is exactly the kind of consideration that belongs in the inspection, not in a brochure.

What does it deliver? The benefits at a glance

The benefits of a renovation to label A can partly be expressed in money and partly not. We deliberately describe them qualitatively here: exact amounts, percentages and payback periods differ per home and per situation, and depend on energy prices and your own usage. For specific figures and any schemes, we refer you to official sources such as the central government (Rijksoverheid), or to an independent adviser.

What we can say with confidence is that the combination of a good envelope and modern installations makes a difference on several fronts.

  • Lower energy costs: a more efficient property needs less energy, which usually translates into lower monthly costs
  • More comfort: less draught and cold downdraught, a more stable temperature and a healthier indoor climate
  • Better lettability: an energy-efficient home is more attractive to many tenants and can make the step to a new tenant easier
  • Better saleability: a good label counts for many buyers and their financing in their decision
  • Future-proofing: an upgraded property is better prepared for any tightened requirements and for a market that increasingly values energy efficiency

Upgrading, letting and having it managed

For many owners, an energy upgrade is not an isolated question but part of a bigger choice: keep letting the property yourself, have it managed or sell it in due course. A home brought up to label A is easier to operate in all of those scenarios, because it is more comfortable, more efficient and more attractive to the next occupant.

If, after the upgrade, you would like the property let and managed without doing the day-to-day work yourself, HomeINN can take over the management at fixed percentages agreed in advance. That way the line runs straight through from renovation to operation, with the same party that already knows the property. If you would first like to talk through what makes sense for your specific home, we are happy to think along with you, without obligation.

Frequently asked questions

Should I insulate first or install a heat pump straight away?
In almost all cases, insulate first. A heat pump only comes into its own in a home with a solid envelope and a lower heat demand. The right order differs per property and is something we determine during the inspection.
Do you also quote subsidy amounts and payback periods?
Deliberately not in general terms. They depend heavily on your home, energy prices and the moment. For current schemes and amounts we refer you to the central government (Rijksoverheid) or an independent adviser, and in a personal plan we calculate it for your situation.
Does HomeINN work with a fixed construction partner?
Yes. We have worked with a fixed construction partner for years, so that inspection, planning and execution sit with one coordinated party. That keeps the process clear and helps to stay within the agreed budget.
Can HomeINN also let and manage the property after the upgrade?
Yes. After the renovation we can take over the letting and management at fixed percentages agreed in advance, so that you have no day-to-day work yourself.
